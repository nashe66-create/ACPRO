## Gate 0 — Product Definition

Product Vision, Problem Definition, Actors, Value Proposition, Product Principles, Business Model hypothesis, Decision Log and Assumptions Register.

## Gate 1 — Marketplace Validation

Question: **Do homeowners want this and will qualified installers bid?**

Deliverables: Homeowner research, Installer research, Marketplace hypothesis and Marketplace economics.

## Gate 2 — Product Specification

Every workflow must define actor, trigger, preconditions, actions, decisions, validation, system actions, exceptions, state changes, notifications, data captured and success criteria.

## Gate 3 — Domain Model

Define business objects and relationships before implementation-level database design.

## Gate 4 — State Machines

Formalise Job, Bid and Survey states; later Payment, Finance, Warranty and Service.

## Gate 5 — MVP Technical Engine

Define the Load Estimation Engine before building a manufacturer catalogue.

## Gate 6 — MVP Boundary

Create a hard contract for what is and is not in MVP.

## Gate 7 — Build Specification

Only after workflows and domain definitions are sufficiently stable: screens, forms, database, APIs, authentication, notifications, infrastructure and QA.

## Gate 8 — MVP Build

Homeowner job → load estimate → installers → bids → compare → choose → survey → final quote → installation → review. Manual operations behind the scenes are acceptable.

## Gate 9 — Pilot

Measure demand, supply, liquidity, conversion, economics, operational effort and quality.

## Gate 10 — Post-MVP

Use pilot evidence to determine which automation creates the highest value.
